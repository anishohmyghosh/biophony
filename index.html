    What this is

    A live view of the planet's biophony: the combined sound of living things. Thousands of free, always-on acoustic monitors stream what they recognise, and satellite layers add the view from orbit, so you can compare what a place sounds like with how healthy it looks.

    Listening

    • BirdWeather: over 10,000 stations (BirdWeather PUCs and BirdNET-Pi) running the BirdNET model. Birds, plus bats, insects, frogs and some mammals. Each recognition comes with the original audio clip.
    • Orcasound: live hydrophones in the Salish Sea. Whale calls, vessels and other sounds are flagged by listeners and by the OrcaHello model, and you can listen to the live stream.

    From orbit

    • EMIT (imaging spectrometer on the ISS, 285 bands, 60 m): for each site, the most recent scenes covering it, with cloud cover, a preview and the full reflectance data. EMIT only covers ±52° latitude and passes irregularly. Methane plume detections can be shown on the map.
    • MODIS NDVI: plant greenness on land. PACE OCI chlorophyll: phytoplankton in the ocean. VIIRS: true colour.

    The biodiversity readout

    • Richness: number of species recognised in the last 24 hours.
    • Shannon diversity (H′): higher when there are many species and none dominates.
    • Evenness (J′): H′ divided by its maximum. Near 1 means detections are spread evenly; near 0 means one or two species dominate.

    These describe what one microphone heard, not the whole ecosystem. Recognitions can be wrong (BirdNET is strongest on North American and European birds), station hardware differs, and a busy feeder or a noisy road changes everything. Treat them as signals to compare over time, not as a health score.
